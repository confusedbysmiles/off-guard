# Nine Minutes to the Toast

*A Pathfinder Second Edition one-shot*

**Three characters, 12th level. Roughly four hours. Puzzle-forward, comic register.**

---

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Party | 3 PCs, level 12, player-built |
| Runtime | 4 hours, structured in 9-minute in-fiction loops |
| Combats | 1 recurring trivial, 1 negotiation-with-a-stat-block, 1 moderate, 1 severe boss |
| XP budget | 3-PC party: Trivial 30 / Low 40 / Moderate 60 / Severe 90 / Extreme 120 |
| Skills rewarded | Diplomacy, Society, Thievery, Deception, Perception, Crafting, any Lore |
| Books needed | Rage of Elements, Monster Core 2, NPC Core, GM Core, Pathfinder #154 |

**On sourcing.** Every stat block and DC-setting rule below is quoted from a published
page and cited in §13. Two numbers are my own GM rulings rather than published values,
and both are labelled as such where they appear: the **effect rank of the wish** (§12) and
the **rank of the aspic's preservation magic** (§5). Everything else is quoted.

Of the five books above, your Drive has Rage of Elements, Monster Core 2, NPC Core and GM
Core. *Pathfinder #154: Siege of the Dinosaurs* supplies only the Tallow Ooze, which is
reproduced in full in §6, so you do not need it.

---

## 1. The premise (GM only)

In **4413 AR**, **Corwin Vairemont**, third Duke of that name, retained a noble ifrit
through a broker who did not read the fine print. What Corwin wished for was this: *that
no Vairemont betrothal feast should ever be less than perfect.*

The ifrit granted it. Pedantically. This is what noble ifrits do — the Ifrit Shuyookh
stat block carries a **Pedantic Wish** ability whose published text says it "subverts the
wish-maker's true intentions, twisting the phrasing to maximize suffering." Corwin got
exactly what he asked for and none of what he meant.

There has not been a Vairemont betrothal in three hundred and twelve years.

Tonight there is one. The ifrit — **Qazrahin the Fastidious** — has woken, reviewed the
contract, found it binding, and gone to work.

The feast is not perfect. Qazrahin has therefore burned the dining room to ash and
rewound the evening. Repeatedly. It has stopped counting how many times, which frightens
it more than it will admit to anyone, including itself.

**The tone note that matters most:** Qazrahin is not evil, not mad, and not a mastermind.
It is a contractor with a spec it cannot meet and no authority to renegotiate. Play it as
competent, exhausted, and unfailingly polite. It apologizes before each incineration. It
means the apology.

*Dates assume a present of 4725 AR. Shift all four years by the same offset if your table
runs a different date.*

## 2. The loop

**7:51 PM.** The soup is being cleared. The party sits at the far end of the table.

**8:00 PM.** Duke Ansel rises and raises his glass. Something is wrong. Qazrahin
incinerates the dining room. Everything resets to 7:51.

Nine minutes. The party remembers. Almost nobody else does.

### Why the party remembers

The wish protects the *feast*, and the feast's protected participants are its *guests*.
The party are hired staff. They are not on the guest list. The contract classes them as
"furnishings and attendant persons."

Two consequences, both load-bearing:

- **The Guest List Trap.** A PC who gets themselves seated as a guest becomes protected,
  and therefore starts forgetting. See §7.
- **Havel remembers.** The butler is staff too. See §4. This is the single best card in
  your hand and you should not play it early.

### The clock: how to run nine minutes at the table

Do not track feet or seconds. The loop is **nine slots**, one per minute.

- **Each PC gets one meaningful action per slot** — talking to a guest, searching a
  sideboard, picking a lock, casting a spell with a casting time under a minute, moving
  to another room.
- **Moving between the dining room and any other location costs one slot**, each way. The
  manor is not large; the cost is social, not physical. Leaving the table is conspicuous.
- **Combat costs one slot per five rounds**, minimum one. Run the fight in normal
  encounter mode, then spend the slots.
- **Slot 7 (7:57) is when the aspic goes off**, every loop, without exception.
- **End of slot 9: the toast, then the fire.** Reset.

Nine minutes is ninety rounds, so distance is genuinely not the constraint at this level —
a PC with Speed 25 covers over two thousand feet in the window. The constraint is
attention. Three PCs across nine slots is twenty-seven actions per loop, and there is
comfortably four times that much to do.

**The puzzle is allocation, not discovery.** Say that to your players once, out loud,
after their second loop. It is the most useful sentence in this document.

### What carries over and what does not

| Carries over | Resets |
|---|---|
| PC memories, knowledge, notes, plans | Everyone else's memories (except Havel) |
| Influence *discoveries* about the Duke | Influence *Points* — but see §5 |
| A dead Carnivorous Blob (§6) | All NPC positions and states |
| | PC HP, spell slots, focus points, conditions |
| | PC inventory, including anything picked up |
| | All damage, deaths, and destroyed property |
| | The aspic, the wine, the guest count, the Duke's speech |

**PCs reset to full.** Full HP, all slots, all focus, all consumables restored, conditions
cleared, and anything they looted returns to where it was. Say this plainly after the
first reset. It converts the loop from a horror premise into a playground, and it is the
mechanical permission slip that makes players try absurd things — which is the entire
point of the session.

It also means **the party cannot win by attrition and cannot lose by dying.** Death is a
wasted loop, nothing more. Lean into that. The only real resource is loops, and the only
real currency is information.

### How many loops

Budget **six to nine**. Loop 1 is bewilderment. Loops 2 through 5 are investigation and
should feel productive. Loop 6 or 7 is usually the attempted perfect run, which fails on
one fault they underestimated. The loop after that is the real one.

If you reach hour three with fewer than three faults solved, use §10.

## 3. The house

Six locations, each one slot from the dining room.

**The Dining Room** — the hub. Twelve people at an eleven-person table, a sideboard
bearing the aspic, a chandelier, and a dozen ice buckets, water carafes, and coolers of
chilled wine. **Note those and describe them in loop 1.** They are the boss's published
weakness sitting in plain view for three hours before it matters.

**The Cellar** — down a service stair behind the pantry. Racks, a false wall, and
Great-Aunt Hestia. Cold, dark, and site-bound.

**The Kitchen** — chaos, a head chef in a state, a door to the cold room.

**The Cold Room** — the walk-in. Gargantuan problem inside. See §6.

**The Gift Room** — off the entry hall. Twelve wrapped gifts, one of which contains a
professionally forged letter of introduction for a baron who does not exist.

**The Orangery** — glass-roofed, full of citrus trees that should not fruit in this season
and do. Qazrahin's anchor. A PC with *truesight*, or a DC 30 Perception check to Seek,
sees a figure of banked fire on an iron bench with a ledger, updating it.

Let them find Qazrahin early. It is not a solution and it costs a slot. It will speak
courteously, confirm almost everything, and refuse to help, because helping is not in the
contract. This is a great scene and it accelerates nothing.

## 4. The table

**Eleven legitimate guests. Twelve people seated.** Count them aloud, in front of the
players, every loop.

1. **Duke Ansel Vairemont** — the host. Fault Four.
2. **Iolanthe Vairemont** — the daughter, betrothed, radiant, oblivious.
3. **Teodor Wend** — the fiancé. Nervous, kind, drinks too fast.
4. **Dowager Marchioness Perpetua Wend** — Teodor's mother, and the subject of the line
   the Duke should not say.
5. **Sister Aldeth Corvane** — here to officiate. Quietly doing arithmetic on the seating
   and getting twelve.
6. **Canon Fessimer Oyle** — Aldeth's superior. Says almost nothing, eats enormously,
   misses nothing.
7. **Bellamy Vairemont** — the Duke's brother. Loudly supportive of the match in a way
   suggesting he has money on it failing.
8. **Odile Wend** — Teodor's sister. Bored, sharp, the easiest guest to recruit and the
   fastest route to the Duke.
9. **Lady Marisol Vairemont-Enn** — a cousin newly back from abroad, who has not seen
   Iolanthe since childhood and keeps saying so.
10. **Master Girard Pell** — the family solicitor. **He has a copy of the contract in his
    case.** The most important object in the adventure.
11. **Lady Sabetha Crowe** — a neighbour. Talks continuously about horses. Knows, without
    knowing she knows, that the cellar has a false wall.

**The twelfth chair:** **"Baron Willem Ostrejk."** Fault Two.

### Havel

**Havel** is the butler. Forty years of service. Servant (NPC Core, Creature −1). He is
not a guest, which means he is not protected, which means **he remembers every loop.**

He has remembered all of them. He has served through every incineration with his
composure intact, because the alternative would be a scene.

He will not raise it. It is not his place. If a PC asks him directly — *anything* that
acknowledges the loop, even obliquely — he answers completely and without surprise, and
he is enormously relieved.

**What Havel knows:** the aspic's history and that it comes from the cold room. That the
decanter is wrong and has been for two centuries. That the Duke has rewritten the toast
four times this week. The count of twelve. He knows *what* happens.

**What Havel does not know:** why. He has no theory about the fire, has never heard of
Qazrahin, and has not read the contract. He is a butler, not an investigator.

This keeps him from short-circuiting the adventure while making him the warmest thing in
it. He is also your best hint delivery mechanism, and unlike §10 he costs the party
nothing but the courtesy of asking.

### Statting the table

Guests use **NPC Core** blocks and are never meant to be fought: Noble (Creature 3) for
the aristocrats, Servant (Creature −1) for staff, Palace Guard (Creature 4) for the house
guard, Halfling Head Chef (Creature 2) for the kitchen.

Against level 12 PCs these are scenery. A Creature 4 is **eight levels below the party —
off the bottom of the XP table entirely**, with AC 21 against attack bonuses in the high
twenties. Use them as Influence targets, obstacles and comedy. Never as opposition.

## 5. The four faults

The toast fails for four independent reasons. **All four must be fixed in a single loop.**
This is the spine of the adventure and the reason no single spell breaks it.

### Fault One — The wine is a fake

The ceremonial decanter should hold the **4413 Vairemont Reserve**, bottled the year of
the wish. It holds table wine with a fig in it.

In **4525 AR**, **Great-Aunt Hestia Vairemont** drank the 4413 by herself over a long
weekend and replaced it with something from the village. She then died, became a ghost,
and has spent two centuries guarding the cellar so nobody finds out. She is not
protecting the wine. She is protecting her reputation.

**Discovery:** DC 25 Perception or any relevant Lore to taste or smell it. A crit gets the
vintage mismatch outright. *Read aura* shows the preservation magic absent, which for a
4413 is impossible. Havel confirms it instantly if asked.

**The fix:** the real vintage is behind the false wall.

- Telling Hestia she was wrong: automatic failure. She attacks.
- Offering not to tell anyone: **DC 28 Diplomacy or Deception.** This is the solve.
- Promising the toast will name her: **DC 25**, and the best outcome — she helps in later
  loops and volunteers Fault Three unprompted.

**Encounter:** §6, *The Cellar*.

### Fault Two — Twelve people at an eleven-person table

**"Baron Willem Ostrejk"** is **Piotr Hallow**, a food critic who has spent nine years
trying to get into a Vairemont dinner and has finally simply walked in with a forged
letter. He is having the best night of his life. The contract specifies eleven guests.
Twelve is an imperfection.

**Discovery:** DC 30 Perception or Society to notice the count. DC 32 Deception or
Diplomacy to catch him in conversation — he is genuinely good at this. The forged letter
is in the Gift Room (DC 25 Thievery or Perception). *Zone of truth* exposes him
immediately and you should let it: it solves this fault and only this fault.

**The fix, either route:**

- **Remove him.** He will not go willingly. Blackmail works (DC 28 Intimidation).
  Removing him physically is a slot, a scene, and very funny.
- **Legitimize him.** Get the Duke or Havel to add him to the list, making twelve correct.
  Harder — the Duke's Influence track must be at 4+ — but it is the better ending, and
  Piotr helps them in later loops if they do it.

Piotr does not fight. If cornered he cries, and then he negotiates, and he negotiates
well.

### Fault Three — The dessert is alive

The **Vairemont Ancestral Aspic** has been magically preserved and re-served at every
family occasion since 4413. It is no longer food.

At **7:57 every loop** it heaves off the sideboard as a **tallow ooze**. The guests
politely ignore it, because acknowledging it would embarrass the Duke. Havel serves around
it, because he has done this many times.

**Why you cannot fight your way out of this:** 270 HP, immune to piercing, slashing,
precision and critical hits. A level 12 party with blades cannot meaningfully hurt it, and
it re-forms next loop regardless. Its *Greasy Seepage* aura forces DC 30 Acrobatics checks
and *Residual Grease* makes people drop what they are holding — at a formal dinner, in
front of eleven guests, during the soup.

**The fix, three routes:**

- **Get it out of the room before 7:57.** Two slots of effort plus DC 28 Athletics. Comedy
  peak of the session.
- **Dispel the preservation.** *Dispel magic* against the preservation effect. It collapses
  into three centuries of appropriate decay. **GM ruling: treat the preservation as rank
  4** — this is my number, not a published one; it is set low deliberately so the clever
  answer works. Reward it.
- **Kill the parent** in the cold room. The aspic stops re-forming permanently, in every
  subsequent loop. §6.

### Fault Four — The Duke does not approve

**Duke Ansel** has written a toast. It is warm for four sentences and then it is not. He is
going to say the thing about Perpetua Wend. He knows he should not. He is going to.

This is an **Influence encounter** (GM Core) and it needs one house rule, because the
subsystem's published rounds run fifteen minutes to an hour and the loop is nine minutes
long.

> **House rule.** Inside a loop, **one slot spent on the Duke = one Influence round
> action.** Points reset with the loop — but **any threshold the party has already reached
> in a previous loop can be re-reached by spending a single slot with no check**, because
> they now know exactly what to say. So the final perfect run costs one slot to restore
> all earned thresholds, plus whatever it takes to push higher.

Without that rule the 8-point track is mathematically unreachable in nine minutes. With
it, Fault Four becomes what it should be: expensive the first time, cheap once solved.

**Duke Ansel Vairemont — Influence**

*Noncombat level 12. Perception +20; Will +22.*

**Discovery** DC 30 Society, DC 28 Perception, DC 25 any Lore (Heraldry, Genealogy, Wine)

**Influence Skills** DC 25 Lore (Wine), DC 28 Crafting, DC 28 Intimidation,
DC 30 Diplomacy, DC 32 Performance

**Influence Thresholds**

- **2 points** — he admits the speech "may run long."
- **4 points** — he shows them the written toast, and will add a guest to the list if asked
  now. (The hook into Fault Two.)
- **6 points** — he explains the Perpetua problem: a thirty-year-old business betrayal he
  has never said out loud and which Iolanthe knows nothing about.
- **8 points** — he accepts a replacement line. **Fault Four is fixed.**

**Resistance** Any appeal on the grounds of *Iolanthe's sake* — **+5 DC.** He has been told
this all week and it is why he has dug in.

**Weakness** Wine, the estate, or the 4413 specifically — **−5 DC.** He would much rather
discuss the cellar than his feelings.

**A party that solves Fault One first gets Fault Four at a discount**, because they arrive
with something he actually wants to talk about. That dependency is deliberate. Do not
signpost it; do reward it.

Note the subsystem's own design advice: Diplomacy belongs on the list but should rarely be
the best skill. Discovering is what pays.

## 6. Encounters

Budgets for a **three-PC party at level 12**. The standard four-PC budgets are Trivial 40
/ Low 60 / Moderate 80 / Severe 120 / Extreme 160; one missing character subtracts that
tier's Character Adjustment (10 / 20 / 20 / 30 / 40), giving:

**Trivial 30 / Low 40 / Moderate 60 / Severe 90 / Extreme 120.**

### The Aspic — trivial by XP, expensive by nature

**Tallow Ooze, Creature 11** — *Pathfinder #154: Siege of the Dinosaurs* pg. 83 — **30 XP,
Trivial.**

Medium, mindless, ooze. Perception +14; motion sense 60 ft, no vision. Athletics +23.
Str +6, Dex −5, Con +5, Int −5, Wis +0, Cha −5.
**AC 19; Fort +22, Ref +10, Will +13; HP 270.**
Immune acid, critical hits, mental, piercing, precision, slashing, unconscious, visual.
Resist cold 10. **Weak fire 10.** Speed 20, swim 20.

- **Pseudopod** +23, 2d10+10 bludgeoning plus residual grease.
- **Engulf** (2 actions) DC 30, 4d10 bludgeoning, Escape DC 26, Rupture 25.
- **Congealed** (reaction) — fire damage makes it **quickened 1** for a round (Stride or
  Strike only). Cold damage — *even if negated by its resistance* — slows it 1 and
  prevents Engulf for a round.
- **Greasy Seepage** (aura, 10 ft) — DC 30 Acrobatics on entering or starting a turn in
  the area. A creature that Steps does not roll.
- **Residual Grease** — after a crit or an escape from Engulf, DC 30 Reflex when wielding
  items for 1d4 rounds; drop equipment on a critical failure.

**Run it as a social disaster, not a fight.** The guests do not react. Havel serves around
it. Any PC who draws a weapon in the dining room has created a *different* imperfection.
The trap is obvious once stated and nobody sees it coming: **killing the aspic at the
table ruins the toast exactly as thoroughly as the aspic does.**

Note *Congealed*: a fire-damage party makes it faster. That joke is in the published block.

### The Cellar — Great-Aunt Hestia

**Ghost Mage, Creature 10, with the Elite adjustment → Creature 11** — Monster Core —
**30 XP, Trivial.**

Elite values: **AC 29; Fort +18, Ref +21, Will +24; HP 155.** Perception +19; darkvision.
Arcana +24, Intimidation +24, Stealth +23. Ghostly hand +23, 2d8+14 void. Spell DC 31,
attack +25. Fly 25. Incorporeal, spirit, undead.
Immune bleed, death effects, disease, paralyzed, poison, precision, unconscious.
Resist all damage 10 except force, ghost touch, spirit or vitality — **doubled against
non-magical damage.**
**Site Bound.** **Rejuvenation.**
*Frightful Moan* DC 31 Will, 30 ft, frightened 2 (3 on a crit failure).
*Telekinetic Assault* DC 31 basic Reflex, 6d6 bludgeoning, 30-ft emanation.

**Be honest with yourself about this fight: three level-12 PCs will kill her in two or
three rounds.** 155 HP and resistance 10 that magical weapons already halve is not a wall.
She is a **negotiation with a stat block attached**, and the lock is not her hit points —
it is that *killing her does not get them the wine.*

She is the only creature who knows which rack conceals the false wall. Without her: **DC
35 Perception** to find the seam, then a slot and a DC 30 Thievery or Athletics to open it.
That is two extra slots and a hard check, against DC 28 Diplomacy for asking nicely.

Rejuvenation is 2d4 days, so it is the loop reset — not the ability — that brings her
back. She returns next loop unhelpful and, if they killed her, offended: **+5 DC on every
social check with her for the rest of the session.** That is the cost. It is a lesson, not
a punishment.

Use *Frightful Moan* once, for effect.

### The Cold Room — the parent

**Carnivorous Blob, Creature 13** — Monster Core 2 pg. 243 — **60 XP, Moderate.**

Gargantuan, mindless, ooze. Perception +23; motion sense 240 ft, no vision. Athletics +27.
Str +8, Dex −3, Con +6, Int −5, Wis +0, Cha −5.
**AC 20; Fort +25, Ref +14, Will +19; HP 300.**
Immune acid, bleed, critical hits, mental, **piercing**, precision, **slashing**, sonic,
unconscious, visual. Speed 20, climb 20, swim 20.

- **Pseudopod** +25, 2d12+12 bludgeoning plus 2d6 acid, Grab.
- **Constrict** 2d12 bludgeoning plus 2d6 acid, DC 33.
- **Engulf** (2 actions) DC 33, 4d10 acid, Escape DC 33, Rupture 20.
- **Split** — when it has 10 or more HP and is hit by an attack that would deal piercing
  **or** slashing damage, **it splits into two identical oozes, each with half the
  original's HP.** This coexists with its immunity to both: the attack does nothing and
  doubles the problem.
- **Retaliating Strike** (reaction) — on taking damage, Strike an adjacent creature.
- **Acid** — DC 33 Fortitude or drained.

**This is the real combat, and it is a puzzle.** A party that opens with a rapier turns one
Gargantuan ooze into two. Then four. In a walk-in. The answers are bludgeoning, fire,
spells dealing neither piercing nor slashing, or shutting the door and solving Fault Three
another way.

Killing it **permanently stops the aspic re-forming in every subsequent loop.** It is the
only progress that survives a reset. Tell them it stuck — they will feel that win
enormously.

*One flag: some printings describe the blob's acid as damaging flesh only, not bone, stone
or wood. I could not confirm that clause on the current Nethys page, so treat it as
flavour rather than a ruling.*

### Optional — The Kitchen

If you need a fourth fight, the fire in the range is one of Qazrahin's bound staff.

**Magma Scorpion, Creature 8** — Monster Core 2 pg. 149 — **10 XP each.**
**AC 27; Fort +19, Ref +14, Will +16; HP 155.** Perception +18; darkvision, smoke vision.
Immune bleed, fire, paralyzed, poison, sleep. **Weak cold 10.** Speed 40, climb 30.
Pincer +20, 2d6+9 bludgeoning plus 1d6 persistent fire and Grab (reach 10 ft, agile).
Tail sting +20, 1d10+9 piercing plus 1d6 persistent fire plus venom (DC 26 Fort).
Magma spit +17 ranged 40 ft, 1d6+9 fire plus 1d6 persistent fire.

Four is 40 XP, exactly Low. They are stupid (Int −4) and not plot-critical. Cut freely.

## 7. The Guest List Trap

If a PC gets themselves seated as a guest — and someone will try, because it is the obvious
way to reach the Duke — **they stop being furniture and start being protected.**

At the next reset, that PC loses their memories along with everyone else.

Play it straight and do not warn them. The other two wake into loop N+1 with a friend who
does not know them. It is genuinely unsettling for about ninety seconds, then it is very
funny, and it is recoverable: get them off the list, or re-brief them, which costs two
slots and is worth every second.

Havel can re-brief them in one slot, if anyone thinks to ask him. Most tables do not, and
then someone realises he could have all along.

**Do not do this twice.** Once is a memorable beat. Twice is a GM with a grudge.

## 8. The boss

Fix all four faults in one loop and the toast lands. The room goes silent. It is perfect.

And then Qazrahin does not let it happen.

A completed contract is a dismissed contractor. Qazrahin has been awake for one evening
after three hundred and twelve years of nothing, and it would rather burn the room again
than go back. It becomes visible at 8:00, apologizes, and reaches for the fire.

### Route A — Fight it

**Ifrit Shuyookh, Creature 14** — *Rage of Elements* pg. 129 — **80 XP**
**plus Magma Scorpion, Creature 8** — Monster Core 2 — **10 XP**
**= 90 XP, Severe.** Exactly on budget for three level-12 PCs.

Rare, Large, elemental, fire, genie. Perception +25; darkvision. Common, Pyric; constant
*truespeech*. Arcana +25, Athletics +28, Crafting +23, Deception +25, Diplomacy +24,
Intimidation +26, Society +23. Str +8, Dex +4, Con +5, Int +3, Wis +3, Cha +5.

**AC 36; Fort +26, Ref +23, Will +27; HP 300.**
**Immune fire. Weak cold 15, water 15.**
+2 striking scimitar, +1 resilient breastplate. Speed 25, fly 35.

- **Scimitar** +31, 2d6+16 slashing plus 4d6 fire (fire, forceful, magical, reach 10 ft,
  sweep).
- **Fist** +29, 1d4+16 bludgeoning plus 4d6 fire (agile, magical, reach 10 ft).
- **Heat of Blazing Wings** (aura, 5 ft) — 4d6 fire, DC 31 basic Reflex.
- **Burning Grasp** — grabbed creatures take 4d6 fire immediately and at the end of each
  of their turns until they escape.
- **Combat Grab** — one-action Strike that grabs on a hit.
- **Exploit Regret** (reaction) — when a creature misses an attack, Qazrahin offers it the
  chance to express regret. If accepted, Qazrahin takes the damage and becomes quickened
  for 1d4 rounds.
- **Spells** DC 35, attack +27. **7th:** *interplanar teleport* (at will), *volcanic
  eruption*. **5th:** *fire's pathway*, *fireball* (at will). **4th:** *invisibility* (×2).
  **Cantrips (7th):** *detect magic*, *ignition*, *illuminate*.
- **Change Shape** — Small or Medium fire elemental or reptile.

**The room is the answer.** Weak cold 15 and water 15, in a dining room stocked with ice
buckets, water carafes and coolers of chilled wine — all described in loop 1, three hours
before it matters. A thrown bucket is an improvised weapon, but 15 points of weakness
damage per hit is real, and a party that works this out has earned a great deal. If nobody
has noticed by round three, have Havel calmly hand a PC an ice bucket without comment.

*Exploit Regret* is the jewel of the block. Qazrahin, mid-fight, politely offering to eat a
hit in exchange for going faster, is exactly the register of this adventure. Use it every
round it is available.

**Do not use *interplanar teleport* to flee.** It has it at will and a boss that escapes
ruins the ending. Qazrahin will not leave. Leaving is not in the contract.

### Route B — Break the contract

**Master Girard Pell has a copy in his case.** A PC who gets it and spends a slot reading
it, with DC 28 Society or any legal Lore, finds the clause:

> the contract binds *betrothal* feasts. It says nothing about *wedding* feasts.

If the toast is delivered as a **wedding** toast rather than a betrothal toast, the contract
was never engaged tonight, and Qazrahin is discharged having technically never failed. Its
record remains spotless. This matters to it enormously.

Sister Aldeth is present and can officiate on the spot. Iolanthe and Teodor need convincing
(DC 28 Diplomacy each, or one check if Odile is already an ally). The Duke must be at 6
Influence Points to permit it.

Route B is hard to *find* and trivial to *execute*, and it ends with the ifrit shaking
everyone's hand. Both routes are real endings. Neither is the good one.

### If they do neither

Qazrahin resets the room and play continues. There is no fail state. The adventure ends
when the players decide it does, or when you are out of time and reach for §10.

## 9. The four-hour run sheet

| Block | Clock | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0–15 | Straight into dinner. No briefing, no premise. Soup, guests, the count, the aspic on the sideboard, the ice buckets. |
| Loop 1 | 15–32 | Let it run. Aspic at 7:57. Toast at 8:00. Fire. Reset. **Explain nothing.** |
| Loop 2 | 32–52 | Explain the clock and the reset rules when they ask. Establish that PCs reset to full. Let them test the loop's edges. |
| Loops 3–5 | 52–135 | The investigation body. Cellar, kitchen, gift room, orangery. Expect two faults identified, one solved. Cold Room fight lands here. |
| Loops 6–7 | 135–180 | The attempted perfect run. It should fail on the fault they underestimated — usually Four. |
| The real loop | 180–205 | All four fixed. The toast lands. |
| Boss | 205–232 | Route A or Route B. |
| Wrap | 232–240 | The morning after. One line each on what their PC does with the memory of an evening that officially happened once. |

**Cut points, in the order to cut them:**

1. **The optional Kitchen combat.** Gone without a trace.
2. **The Gift Room.** Move the forged letter onto Piotr; DC 28 Thievery at the table.
3. **The Orangery.** Atmospheric, not structural.
4. **Fault Two.** Sister Aldeth exposes Piotr unprompted at the start of loop 5 — she has
   been doing the arithmetic all evening. Costs the party nothing, saves fifteen minutes.
5. **The Cold Room.** Let *dispel magic* or physical removal handle the aspic.

**Do not cut** Fault Four or the boss. Fault Four is where the adventure has a heart, and
the boss is the payoff for four faults of setup.

## 10. The pressure valve

If you hit hour three with fewer than three faults solved, Qazrahin cracks.

It appears in the dining room mid-loop, sits down in the twelfth chair, and says it has
been keeping a list. It hands over the ledger. The ledger names all four faults, in order,
in a very neat hand, with tally marks beside each for the number of loops that fault has
personally ruined.

It is not helping. It is complaining. This is the worst evening of its professional life
and it needs someone to know.

Then it takes the ledger back, apologizes, and resets the room.

This converts a stalled investigation into a clean execution problem without taking
anything from the players, and it is completely in character. Deploy without hesitation.

**Softer valve first:** have Havel refill someone's glass and say something that only makes
sense if he remembers. It costs you nothing and it is a better scene.

## 11. Handouts

**Handout 1 — The seating card.** The eleven legitimate names from §4, in order. Hand it
out in loop 1. Twelve people will be at the table. The discrepancy sits in their hands all
session.

**Handout 2 — The Duke's toast.** Four warm sentences and then the line about Perpetua.
Give it at Influence threshold 4.

**Handout 3 — The contract, clause 3(a).** One deliberately dry paragraph using the word
*betrothal* four times and *wedding* never. Give it when they get Pell's case.

Handout 3 is the one that matters. Do not paraphrase it aloud — hand them paper and let
someone spot the gap. A player going quiet and re-reading a legal paragraph is the best
thirty seconds this adventure has to offer.

## 12. Contingencies — the level 12 problem

Three 12th-level PCs have real tools. The defence is structural: **four independent faults
mean no single spell wins the session.** Every solve below is real, and each solves exactly
one fault.

| They cast | What happens |
|---|---|
| *detect magic*, *read aura* | The room reads as saturated with one enormous effect. A clue, not a solve. On the decanter: reveals the absent preservation. Fault One discovery, free. |
| *dispel magic* | Works on the aspic's preservation. **Solves Fault Three.** Does nothing to the loop — see the ruling below. |
| *zone of truth* | **Solves Fault Two** outright. Let it. Good use of a slot. |
| *mind reading* | Gets the toast's content from the Duke. Fault Four *discovery*, not fix — knowing the line does not change it. |
| *interplanar teleport* | They leave. The reset returns them. The loop is temporal, not spatial. Excellent loop-2 discovery. |
| *teleport* | **Cannot be cast in the loop at all** — 10-minute casting time against a nine-minute window. Worth letting a player discover the hard way once. |
| *truesight* | Sees Qazrahin. Costs a slot, solves nothing, wonderful scene. |
| *divination*, *read omens* | Give a real one-sentence answer, honestly. It costs a slot and should pay. |
| *speak with dead* | Unnecessary. Hestia is right there and extremely talkative. |
| Raise or resurrect anything | Irrelevant. The reset undoes all deaths. |
| Flight, invisibility, dimensional movement | Fine. Distance was never the constraint. |
| *freeze time* (legacy *time stop*) | Rank 10. Unavailable at 12. This is a reason the level is 12 and not 15. |

**The one ruling to hold firm on.** The loop is not dispellable, counterable or negotiable
by magic. **GM ruling: treat the wish as a rank 10 effect.** This is my number, not a
published one — Pedantic Wish is printed as a downtime ritual with no effect rank, so the
rank is mine to set. It is defensible arithmetically: a level 12 caster's highest slot is
rank 6, and a critical success on *dispel magic* counteracts up to rank 9, so rank 10 is
genuinely out of reach rather than arbitrarily denied.

If a player argues, the honest answer is that beating the loop is the adventure, and the
four faults are the mechanism.
