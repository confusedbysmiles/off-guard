# Nine Minutes to the Toast — the run script

*A beat-by-beat walkthrough. Read-aloud text is in quote blocks; everything else
is for you.*

This is the lazy-GM version of the adventure document. The adventure doc explains
why things are the way they are; this one tells you what to say. If they
disagree, the adventure doc is right and this one has a typo.

---

## The spine

Everything after this section is reference — what is in the cellar, how the Duke
works, what Piotr says when he is caught. This section is the route through it.

**What the evening is actually about.** A contractor who cannot stop, and a table
of people who are given one evening that only they will remember. The comedy is
the mechanism and not the point. Somewhere around the fourth loop the party stops
solving a puzzle and starts noticing that Qazrahin has been alone with this since
4413, and that Havel has been alone with it since seven fifty-one.

Nothing in the adventure asks you to underline that. It asks you not to get in
its way.

### The ten beats

Ten moments carry the session. Everything else is texture. Each one has a
trigger, a way to tell it landed, and a lever for when it has not — because a
beat that does not land is not a beat the party failed, it is one you have to
hand them.

**Act one — the trap closes. Loops 1 to 2.**

**1. The soup is fine.**
*Cold open.* Establish an evening so ordinary it is slightly boring. The reset
needs something to break, and a table that never relaxed has nothing to lose.
**Landed when:** a player asks a question about the food, the seating or a guest
rather than about the job.
**If it has not:** slow down. Describe one more course. You cannot skip this.

**2. The room burns.**
*End of loop 1, 8:00 exactly.* Cut them off mid-action if you have to.
**Landed when:** somebody says something out loud that is not in character.
**If it has not:** you did it right anyway. Say the reset line and wait.

**3. Nobody else remembers.**
*Loop 2, early.* The rule that turns this from a disaster into a game.
**Landed when:** a player deliberately tests it — says something outrageous to a
guest to see what happens.
**If it has not, by mid loop 2:** have the Duke greet them again with the exact
same handshake and the exact same sentence, word for word, and deliver it
identically. Repeating yourself verbatim is the cheapest and best tell there is.

**4. You come back whole.**
*Loop 2.* The permission slip, and the most under-used beat in the adventure.
**Landed when:** a player does something reckless on purpose.
**If it has not:** let the aspic hurt someone, then say it flat and out of
character — full hit points, all your slots, everything you spent, everything you
picked up back where it was. Watch what the table does with that. This single
beat is the difference between a cautious session and a good one.

**Act two — the work. Loops 3 to 6.**

**5. There is more to do than there is evening.**
*Loop 3.* The puzzle shows its real shape: not discovery, allocation.
**Landed when:** the players start dividing up the loop between them without
being asked.
**If it has not:** say the sentence out loud. It is in the Loop 2 section and it
is the most useful thing you will say all night.

**6. The four faults have names.**
*Loops 3 to 5.* They do not need solutions yet. They need the list.
**Landed when:** somebody can say all four out loud without checking.
**If it has not, by the end of loop 5:** Sister Aldeth says the count out loud, or
Havel mentions the floor cloths, or you reach for the ledger. In that order.

**7. Havel remembers.**
*Anywhere in loops 3 to 6, once.* The turn. The first moment the loop stops being
a machine and becomes a thing that has been happening to somebody.
**Landed when:** the table goes quiet, or somebody immediately asks him a
question in a different tone of voice.
**If it has not, by loop 6:** use it. Do not save it past six. It is the best card
in the deck and it does nothing in your hand.
**Do not oversell it.** He does not stop moving. He does not sit down. He refills a
glass, says the thing, and carries on, because the alternative would be a scene.

**8. Something sticks.**
*Whenever the thing in the cold room dies.* The only permanent progress in the
adventure.
**Landed when:** you tell them it stuck and somebody reacts.
**If they have not found the cold room by loop 5:** Havel mentions where the aspic
comes from. He knows. He has always known.
**Say it out of character.** "That one stayed dead. The aspic will not re-form —
not this loop, not any loop." A permanent win that the table does not notice is a
permanent win you wasted.

**Act three — the price. The real loop, and after.**

**9. It works, and it is not enough.**
*The perfect run.* All four fixed, the toast lands, the room is silent, and
nothing happens — which is the first time all evening that nothing has happened.
Hold it. Let them believe it.
Then Qazrahin becomes visible and apologises.
**Landed when:** somebody groans, or laughs, or says "no."
**Do not soften this.** It is the reversal the whole session is built on, and a
version where the toast simply works is a worse evening for everyone.

**10. The choice.**
*The boss.* Fight it, or discharge it. Both are real endings and neither is the
good one.
**Landed when:** they pick, and it feels like a decision rather than a default.
**If they have not found the contract:** Pell has been at the table all night with
his case. Have him set it down and go to find the necessary.

### The escalation curve

What the table should feel, loop by loop. When the register is wrong, that is
your signal — not the clock.

| Loop | The table feels | Watch for | If it is not happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bored, then ambushed | Someone breaking character at the fire | Nothing. The fire does this by itself. |
| 2 | Testing the walls | A deliberate experiment on an NPC | Repeat one of your own lines verbatim |
| 3 | Competent | Dividing the loop between them | Say the allocation sentence out loud |
| 4–5 | Working the problem | Three faults named, one solved | Aldeth, then Havel, then the ledger |
| 6–7 | Confident, then wrong | An attempted perfect run that fails | Let it fail. Do not rescue it. |
| 8+ | Grim and precise | Silence, checklists, short sentences | This is the good part. Stay out of the way. |
| Boss | Elated, then robbed | Somebody looking at an ice bucket | Havel hands one over without comment |

**The most common failure is not being stuck — it is loop six going well.** If
their first perfect run succeeds, they have skipped beat 9 and the session ends
forty minutes early on a flat note. If they are close to all four in loop six,
find the fault they have handled most carelessly and let it come apart. The Duke
is usually the one.

### Momentum levers

Keyed to the symptom rather than the clock. Reach for the smallest one that fits.

| The symptom | The lever |
|---|---|
| They have not left the dining room | Havel, quietly, about having the floor cloths ready by ten to |
| They are talking about the wine and getting nowhere | Lady Sabetha mentions the cellar has "always been an awkward shape" |
| Nobody has approached the Duke | Odile Wend approaches *them*. She is bored, she has clocked that they are up to something, and she wants in. |
| They have not counted the table | Sister Aldeth, out loud, to nobody: "That is twelve." |
| They killed Hestia and are stuck | She is back next loop, colder. The +5 DC is the lesson; do not also block the wall. |
| They are treating it as a fight | Let them kill the aspic at the table. The fire at 8:00 explains it better than you can. |
| One player is disengaged | Give them Piotr. He will talk about food to anyone, for as long as they let him. |
| They are stuck on all four | The ledger. Do not hold it back past hour three. |
| They are winning too easily | See the note above. Break the Duke. |

### The five sentences

If you get everything else wrong and these five land, the session works.

1. **"The soup is fine."** Every reset. Flat, identical, no inflection. It becomes
   a thing the table says with you.
2. **"The puzzle is allocation, not discovery."** Once, after loop 2, out of
   character.
3. **"The dessert goes at fifty-seven past, sir. I find it best to have the floor
   cloths ready by ten to."** Havel, not stopping, not looking up.
4. **"I have been awake for one evening in three hundred and twelve years."**
   Qazrahin, at the perfect run, and then the apology.
5. **"What do you do with an evening you are the only one who remembers?"** The
   last thing you say. Then stop talking.

## Before anyone arrives

Five minutes of setup, and only the first two matter.

1. **Print handout 1** (the seating card) and have it in your hand at the start.
   Eleven names. There will be twelve people at the table. That gap is the
   adventure's oldest joke and it sits in their hands all evening.
2. **Open the loop console** on the Loop tab. Loop 1, slot 1.
3. Handouts 2 and 3 stay face-down until they're earned.
4. Have the five encounters imported. You will need at most three of them.
5. Decide now that you will not explain the loop. They work it out. It takes
   ninety seconds and it is the best ninety seconds of the session.

**The one thing to hold in your head:** the party are staff, not guests. That is
why they remember. Everything else follows from it.

## The cold open

No session zero, no premise, no "you have been hired by." Start in the soup.

> The soup was fine. The soup was, in fact, excellent, and you would know more
> about it if you had been permitted to sit closer to it.
>
> You are at the far end of a very long table in the Vairemont dining room,
> hired as discreet additional security, which in practice has meant standing
> near a door for two hours and now means being seated where the candlelight
> gives out. There are eleven guests. You are not one of them. The distinction
> has been made politely and about four times.
>
> At the head of the table, Duke Ansel Vairemont is not eating. He has a folded
> paper by his plate and he keeps touching it and then not picking it up.
>
> His daughter Iolanthe is radiant in the way people are when they have decided
> to be. Her fiancé Teodor is drinking rather faster than the evening requires.
> Somebody's aunt is explaining horses.
>
> A butler moves along the table filling glasses without ever appearing to
> arrive anywhere. He is perhaps sixty. He has not stopped moving since you got
> here.
>
> The clock in the hall says seven fifty-one.

Then hand them the seating card and say nothing about it.

**Let them talk for two or three minutes.** They will introduce themselves to
each other, size up the room, and one of them will start counting people. Let
that land or not; it does not matter yet.

### 7:57 — the aspic

Do not build to this. Deliver it as a minor inconvenience.

> On the sideboard, the dessert moves.
>
> It is a great domed aspic, taller than a wedding cake, the colour of a bruise
> going yellow, and it has been on that sideboard since before you arrived. It
> shivers. Then it heaves itself over the lip of the sideboard and onto the
> floor with a sound like a boot coming out of mud.
>
> Nobody at the table looks at it. Lady Sabetha continues to talk about horses.
> The butler steps around it and refills the Duke's glass.

They will react. Let them. Whatever they do takes the rest of the loop, and it
does not matter, because —

### 8:00 — the toast

Cut them off mid-action if you have to. The clock is the clock.

> The Duke stands. The table quiets. He raises his glass, and he unfolds the
> paper, and he does not look at it, because he knows it by heart.
>
> "My daughter," he says, "has always known her own mind."
>
> And then the room is on fire.
>
> Not a fire that spreads. A fire that is simply already everywhere, white at
> the centre, and in the half-second you have you can see that nobody at the
> table has noticed it. Iolanthe is still smiling. Teodor is still holding his
> glass up.
>
> Somewhere very close, a voice you have not heard before says, "I do apologise."

### The reset

This exact text, every loop. It is the spine of the session and it should become
a thing they can say along with you by loop four.

> The soup is fine.
>
> The clock in the hall says seven fifty-one.

Stop there. Let the silence do it.

**Do not explain anything.** Not now, not when they ask, not until loop 2. If a
player says "wait, is this a time loop" — the correct answer is "the soup is
fine." Say it flatly and let them test it.

## Loop 2 — the rules become visible

This is the loop where you hand them the game. Answer questions honestly and
completely when asked, and volunteer nothing.

**What they will establish, in roughly this order:**

- Nobody else remembers. The Duke will introduce himself again with the exact
  same handshake.
- They reset to full. Say this plainly the moment it comes up: full HP, all
  spell slots, all focus points, every consumable back in the pouch, and
  anything they picked up is back where it was. It converts the loop from a
  horror premise into a playground and it is the permission slip that makes
  them try absurd things.
- Nine minutes. Let them time it if they want.
- They can leave the room. It does not help.

**When they ask you how it works, tell them the clock:** nine slots, one
meaningful thing each per slot, one slot to go somewhere and one to come back.
Show them the console if you like. It is not a secret; it is the game.

**The sentence to say out loud, once, after this loop:**

> "The puzzle is allocation, not discovery. Nothing is hidden from you. There is
> just more to do than there is evening."

That single line reframes the whole session and stops the table from hunting for
secret doors.

### The Guest List Trap

If a PC has themselves added to the seating card — and one of them will, because
it is the obvious way to get near the Duke — do not warn them. At the next reset:

> The soup is fine. The clock says seven fifty-one.
>
> [Name] is looking at the two of you with the pleasant, empty expression of
> someone who has never met you.

Play it straight for about ninety seconds. Then it becomes very funny. Getting
them off the list fixes it, and Havel can re-brief them in one slot if anyone
thinks to ask him.

**Once. Never twice.**

## The locations

Each is one slot from the dining room, each way. Read-aloud for arriving, then
what is actually there.

### The cellar

> The service stair behind the pantry goes down further than the house appears
> to allow. It is cold. The racks run off into a dark that your light does not
> finish.
>
> Something is already looking at you. She has been dead for two hundred years
> and she has the bearing of a woman interrupted at her correspondence.
>
> "This," says Great-Aunt Hestia Vairemont, "is not a part of the house for
> guests."

**How to play her:** enormously dignified, entirely defensive, and lying about
something so small that the lie has outlived everyone who would have cared.
She is not guarding wine. She is guarding a reputation.

Her lines, if useful:

> "The cellar is inventoried. It has always been inventoried."

> "I do not know what you are implying and I would like you to imply it more
> quietly."

> "It was a *difficult weekend*."

**The three ways this goes:**

| They say | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| "We won't tell anyone." | 28 Diplomacy or Deception | She moves the rack. This is the solve. |
| "The toast will name you." | 25 | She moves the rack *and* volunteers Fault III unprompted. Best outcome. |
| "You drank it, didn't you." | — | Automatic failure. She attacks. |

If they fight her: she is Creature 11 and three level-12 PCs will kill her in
two or three rounds. Let them. Then:

> The false wall is still a false wall. You do not know which rack.

Finding it alone is DC 35 Perception, then a slot and DC 30 Thievery or
Athletics. And next loop she is back, she remembers nothing, and yet somehow
holds it against them anyway: **+5 DC on every social check with her for the rest
of the session.** Do not explain that penalty. Let them notice she is colder.

### The kitchen and the cold room

> The kitchen is a war. Six people are running and none of them are running into
> each other. The head chef is holding a copper pan and shouting a number.
>
> At the back is a heavy door with frost on the hinges.

Inside the cold room:

> The cold room is the size of a chapel and most of it is occupied.
>
> It has no eyes. It knows you are there. Where the aspic was a dome, this is a
> tide, filling the floor and a good deal of the ceiling, and at the near edge
> of it there is a clean bite taken out where something calved away.

**This is the real fight and it is a puzzle.** Say the following out loud the
first time someone draws a blade, as a Nature check result or just as description:

> It splits. Where the sword went in there are now two of them, each half the
> size, and both of them are moving.

Bludgeoning, fire, or spells that are neither piercing nor slashing. Or shut the
door and solve Fault III another way.

**When it dies, say this plainly and out of character:**

> "That one stuck. The aspic will not re-form. Not this loop, not any loop."

They have earned the only permanent thing in the adventure and they should
absolutely know it.

### The gift room

> Twelve gifts, wrapped, on a long table. The card on the largest reads
> *With the compliments of Baron Willem Ostrejk*, in a hand that has been
> practising.

DC 25 Thievery or Perception turns up the forged letter of introduction. It is
very good work. It is also nine years of work, and that shows.

### The orangery

> The glass roof holds the last of the light. The citrus trees are fruiting,
> which they should not be doing in this season, and they are doing it
> enthusiastically.
>
> On an iron bench, a figure of banked fire sits with a ledger open on its knee.
> It is updating the ledger. It does not stop when you come in.
>
> "Good evening," says Qazrahin the Fastidious. "You will be the staff."

**How to play Qazrahin — the most important voice in the adventure.** Not evil,
not mad, not a mastermind. A contractor with a specification it cannot meet and
no authority to renegotiate. Competent, exhausted, unfailingly polite. It
apologises before each incineration and it means the apology.

It will confirm almost everything and help with nothing, because helping is not
in the contract.

> "I am contracted to ensure that no Vairemont betrothal feast is less than
> perfect. This one is not perfect. I am therefore obliged to try again."

> "Three hundred and twelve years. I was not asked to wait. I was asked to
> ensure. There is a difference and I have had a long time to consider it."

> "No. I am sorry. That would be assistance, and assistance is not among my
> obligations. If it were, I assure you we would both be finished by now."

> "How many times? I stopped counting. I am not proud of that. A professional
> keeps count."

That last line should land quietly. It is the only moment the adventure asks you
to play it straight.

## The four faults, in play

### Fault I — the wine

**Discovery is cheap.** DC 25 Perception or any relevant Lore on the decanter:

> It is wine. It is not *good* wine. There is a fig in the bottom of the
> decanter, which is either a garnish or a crime.

*Read aura* shows the preservation magic is simply absent, which for a bottle
that old is impossible. Havel confirms it instantly if anyone asks him.

### Fault II — the twelfth guest

Count the table out loud, in front of them, every single loop. Somebody will
eventually say "hang on."

**Piotr, once caught:**

> He looks at you for a moment. Then his shoulders come down about two inches
> and he stops being a baron.
>
> "Nine years," he says. "Nine years I have been trying to get into this house.
> Do you have any idea what the kitchen here is capable of? Did you *have* the
> soup?"

He does not fight. If cornered he cries, and then he negotiates, and he
negotiates extremely well. He is having the best night of his life and it would
be a genuine cruelty to end it, which is the point: the merciful solve — getting
him legitimately added to the list — is also the better one, and it needs the
Duke at 4 influence.

### Fault III — the aspic

Covered above. Three routes: remove it before 7:57, dispel the preservation
(rank 4, my ruling), or kill the parent in the cold room.

**The trap nobody sees coming:** killing the aspic *at the table*, in front of
eleven guests, during the soup, ruins the toast exactly as thoroughly as the
aspic does. If a PC draws a weapon in the dining room, do not stop them. Let it
run all the way to 8:00 and let the fire explain it.

### Fault IV — the Duke

This is where the adventure has a heart, so give it room.

**He is not a villain and he is not being stubborn for fun.** Thirty years ago
Perpetua Wend did something to him in a business matter that he has never once
said out loud, and now her son is marrying his daughter, and he has written four
sentences of warmth followed by one sentence he cannot stop himself from saying.

**The influence track, and what he actually gives you:**

| Points | What happens |
|---|---|
| 2 | "It may run a little long. Speeches do." |
| 4 | He shows you the paper. Hand them handout 2. He will also add a guest to the list if asked *now*. |
| 6 | He tells you about Perpetua. This is the scene. |
| 8 | He accepts a different line. Fault IV fixed. |

At 6, slow down and play it:

> "You will think it is about money. It was about money. That is what makes it
> so —" He stops. He turns the glass around once. "She was at my wife's funeral.
> She was very kind at my wife's funeral. And eleven days later she did what she
> did, and she had already decided to do it while she was being kind."
>
> "I have never said that to anyone. I would appreciate it if that continued."

**The weakness is wine, the estate, or the 4413** — five DC lower. He would
vastly rather discuss the cellar than his feelings. A party that solved Fault I
first arrives with something he actually wants to talk about, and gets Fault IV
at a discount. Do not tell them that. Do reward it.

**The resistance is anything on the grounds of Iolanthe's sake** — five DC
higher. He has been told that all week by everyone and it is exactly why he has
dug in.

**The house rule that makes this work:** one slot on the Duke is one influence
action. Points reset with the loop, but any threshold they have already reached
comes back for a single slot with no check, because they know what to say now.
Without that the eight-point track is unreachable in nine minutes.

## Dubious Knowledge in play

Your player took the feat. This is the adventure it was written for and did not
know it.

**The mechanic:** on a failure that is not a critical failure, they get the
correct answer and an erroneous one, undifferentiated.

**Why it is extraordinary here:** every other table lives with that ambiguity
forever. Yours can resolve it — by spending a loop testing it. The feat stops
being a drawback and becomes a resource with a price, and the price is the only
currency the adventure has.

**Delivery, and this is the whole craft of it:** both halves flat, in the same
breath, in the same tone. No pause between them. No "and also." No glance. The
moment you lean on one, the feat is dead for the rest of the session and the
player will never trust a pair again.

The twelve prepared pairs are in the loop console under Recall Knowledge, and in
the adventure document. Every lie is testable inside one loop, and being wrong
never costs more than one loop.

**The two worth waiting for:**

*The Vairemont family, DC 26.* True: no Vairemont betrothal in three centuries.
Erroneous: the family traditionally seats thirteen, for luck. It argues for
*adding* a guest when the fault is that there are already twelve — and the
seating card in their hand says eleven. If they act on it, they have made the
evening worse in a way that is entirely their own fault and completely
understandable.

*The fire, DC 30.* True: the fire is the mechanism of the rewind, not a fire.
Erroneous: anyone out of the dining room at 8:00 is outside the effect. They
will stand in the hall at 7:59 feeling clever. They will wake up at 7:51.

**If they ask you which one was true:** "You have no way to tell." Every time.
Cheerfully.

## The perfect run

When all four faults are fixed in one loop, the console lights up and the room
changes. Slow everything down.

> The aspic is not on the sideboard. The decanter holds something that smells
> like a cellar and a century. There are eleven people at the table.
>
> The Duke stands. He raises his glass. He does not unfold the paper, because he
> left it upstairs.
>
> "My daughter," he says, "has always known her own mind. I have not always been
> glad of it. I am glad of it tonight."
>
> He sits down. Somebody starts to clap.
>
> And nothing happens. Which is, you realise, the first time this evening that
> nothing has happened.

Hold that for a beat. Let them think they have won.

> The fire arrives more slowly this time. Almost reluctantly.
>
> "I know," says Qazrahin, becoming visible at the foot of the table, and it
> sounds tired in a way that fire should not be able to sound. "I know. It was
> perfect. I watched all of it."
>
> "I have been awake for one evening in three hundred and twelve years."
>
> "I do apologise."

## The boss

### Route A — fight it

**Ifrit Shuyookh (Creature 14) plus one Magma Scorpion (Creature 8) — 90 XP,
Severe, exactly on budget for three level-12 PCs.**

**The room is the answer, and you built it in loop 1.** Weak cold 15 and water
15, in a dining room stocked with ice buckets, water carafes and coolers of
chilled wine — all of it described three hours ago as set dressing. A thrown
bucket is an improvised weapon, but fifteen points of weakness damage per hit is
real, and a party that works this out has earned a great deal.

**If nobody has noticed by round three,** have Havel do it without a word:

> Havel steps past you, unhurried, and puts an ice bucket into your hands the
> way he has been handing people things all evening. Then he steps back out of
> the way.

**Use Exploit Regret every round it is available.** It is the jewel of the stat
block: on a miss, Qazrahin politely offers the attacker a chance to express
regret, and if they accept, it takes the damage and becomes quickened. A boss
that gets faster because you were gracious about missing is exactly the register
of this adventure.

> "That was close. Do you regret it?"

**It will not flee.** It has *interplanar teleport* at will and it does not use
it, because leaving is not in the contract.

### Route B — break the contract

Master Girard Pell has a copy in his case. A slot to read it, DC 28 Society or
any legal Lore, and hand them **handout 3**.

The clause binds *betrothal* feasts. It says nothing about *wedding* feasts.

Aldeth can officiate on the spot. Iolanthe and Teodor need convincing — DC 28
Diplomacy each, or one check if Odile is already an ally. The Duke must be at 6
influence to permit it.

> Qazrahin reads it twice. Its finger goes down the page and stops.
>
> "That is a wedding feast," it says. "That is not a betrothal feast at all."
>
> A pause.
>
> "Then I have never failed. My record is intact." It closes the ledger with
> enormous care. "Thank you. I would like to shake your hands, if that is
> permitted. I am told it is a custom."

**Neither route is the good one.** One of them ends with a dead contractor and
one ends with a handshake, and the adventure does not tell you which was better.

---

## The ending

Whichever route, land on the morning after. One line each.

> It is the next morning and it happened once. Officially, entirely, and as far
> as anyone else at that table will ever know, it happened exactly once.
>
> What do you do with an evening you are the only one who remembers?

Let each player answer in a sentence. Do not editorialise. Then stop.

## The emergency table

| It is hour three and… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Fewer than three faults solved | The ledger, below. |
| They are stuck on the Duke | Odile Wend approaches *them*. She is bored and she has worked out that they are up to something, and she wants in. |
| They have not noticed the twelfth guest | Sister Aldeth says it out loud at the start of the next loop. She has been doing the arithmetic all evening. |
| They are stuck on the wine | Havel refills a glass and says "It is not the 4413, sir. It has not been the 4413 for some time." |
| Somebody is not engaged | Give them Piotr. He will talk to anyone about food for as long as they let him. |
| Running long | Cut the kitchen fight, then the gift room, then the orangery. |
| Running very long | Skip to the boss. Qazrahin gives up and attacks at the end of the current loop. |

### The ledger — the pressure valve

Qazrahin appears mid-loop, sits down in the twelfth chair, and puts a book on
the table.

> "I have been keeping a list."
>
> It is a very neat hand. There are four entries. Beside each is a column of
> tally marks, and the columns are not short.
>
> "The wine. The count. The dessert. His Grace's remarks concerning the
> Dowager Marchioness. Four. It has been four the entire time. I have watched
> each of them ruin it more times than I have written down, because at a certain
> point writing it down became worse than not knowing."
>
> It closes the book and stands.
>
> "I am not helping you. I want that understood. I am complaining. There is
> nobody else to complain to and it has been three hundred and twelve years."
>
> "I do apologise."

Then it burns the room.

This turns a stalled investigation into a clean execution problem without taking
anything away from them, and it is completely in character. Deploy it without
hesitation.

### Havel, the softer valve

Before you reach for the ledger, try this. It costs nothing and it is a better
scene.

> Havel refills your glass. You have not touched your glass.
>
> "The dessert goes at fifty-seven past, sir," he says, quietly, not stopping.
> "It always does. I find it best to have the floor cloths ready by ten to."

He remembers. He has always remembered. He has served through every
incineration with his composure intact, because the alternative would be a
scene. He will not raise it himself — it is not his place — but ask him anything
that acknowledges the loop and he answers completely, and he is enormously
relieved.

**What Havel knows:** the aspic's history and where it comes from. That the
decanter is wrong and has been for two centuries. That the Duke has rewritten the
toast four times this week. The count of twelve.

**What Havel does not know:** why. He has never heard of Qazrahin, has not read
the contract, and has no theory. He is a butler, not an investigator.
